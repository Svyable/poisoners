01 / THE EVIDENCE STANDARD

A pathway is not
a verdict.

These crosswalks are research prompts, not a database of sites where contamination has been demonstrated. A source, release, environmental route, contact and health outcome require different kinds of evidence. An inventory can be incomplete; a measured release is not an absorbed dose; and exposure is not automatically injury.

SULFUR / CHEMICAL EVIDENCE ROUTESO₂Sulfur dioxideAIRTRACE THE EVIDENCE +
IDENTITY AND ENVIRONMENTAL BEHAVIOR

Gas-phase SO₂; oxidizes to sulfate aerosol and contributes to sulfuric acid deposition. Do not label every SO₂ ton as sulfuric acid.

Sulfate PM, acid deposition, sensitive lakes, forests and crops.

WHAT IS NOT DEMONSTRATED

Oxidation to sulfate and local exposure are not quantified here; a sector-wide emission estimate does not describe one neighborhood's dose or identify a cause of illness.

THE NEXT USEFUL INVESTIGATION

Find a dated sulfur dioxide observation and distinguish any measured downwind concentration from the emitter's reported mass.

IDENTITY AND ENVIRONMENTAL BEHAVIOR

Emitted elemental, oxidized and particle-bound species have different fates. Aquatic microbes can produce methylmercury; total Hg emissions are not methylmercury releases.

WHAT IS NOT DEMONSTRATED

Emissions of total mercury do not demonstrate methylmercury in a particular fish or person's diet.

THE NEXT USEFUL INVESTIGATION

Seek species-specific stack, deposition, water, and fish-tissue evidence; identify where inorganic mercury might transform into methylmercury.

Sulfuric acid aerosol is not equivalent to emitted SO₂ mass; avoid summing derived sulfate with precursor SO₂ without atmospheric modeling.

SO₂ mass cannot be reclassified or added as directly emitted H₂SO₄ without a justified formation model.

Distinguish a directly emitted acid mist from acid particles formed later in air; seek particle size and concentration measurements.
