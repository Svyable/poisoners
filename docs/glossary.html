TERMS / ACCOUNTING

Climate accounting & measurement

The same unit may describe different accounting boundaries. Read the source and year every time.

Carbon dioxide equivalent (CO₂e)
A common climate unit for expressing the warming effect of different greenhouse gases on a specified time horizon. It is not a measure of chemical toxicity or human dose.
MtCO₂e
One million metric tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent. Producer-linked and national inventories can both use this unit but are not necessarily comparable or additive.
Greenhouse gas (GHG)
A gas that absorbs and emits infrared radiation in the atmosphere; examples include carbon dioxide, methane and nitrous oxide. Climate influence is distinct from local toxic exposure.
Production-linked attribution
Emissions assigned to a fuel or cement producer under a published model, potentially including later use of products sold. The result does not identify an individual release location or a harmed person.
Territorial emissions inventory
Emissions counted within a country's geographic reporting boundary using defined rules. It is different from globally attributed emissions linked to a corporation.
Historical cumulative emissions
Emissions summed over a defined range of past years. A historical total is not an annual rate, and coverage periods can vary across producers.
Scenario
A modeled conditional future outcome based on assumptions. Its outputs are not measured observations, legal findings or guaranteed forecasts.
Data vintage
The reporting year, publication date or model version applicable to a value. An inventory published in 2026 can describe emissions during 2025.
TERMS / SCIENCE

Environmental & exposure science

A release estimate becomes a human-health or ecological-harm claim only after separate evidence bridges the pathway.

Hazard
The capacity of a substance, material or process to cause harm under some conditions. Hazard is different from the actual likelihood of harm at a particular place and dose.
Release
A substance entering an environmental medium such as air, water or soil. A release estimate is not itself evidence of an individual's exposure.
Receptor
A person, organism, population or ecosystem that could be affected by an environmental substance or process.
Exposure pathway
The route by which an emission or substance reaches a receptor—for example inhalation or ingestion—with specific timing, concentration and contact conditions.
Dose
The quantity a receptor takes in or absorbs during a specified period. Dose may depend on air and water concentrations, behavior, physiology and duration.
Dose–response relationship
Evidence or a model connecting exposure amount with a measurable outcome. Interpretation may depend on uncertainty, population differences and confounders.
Fate and transport
Processes that govern how pollutants move, degrade, change form, accumulate or persist between a source and potential receptors.
Attributable burden
The portion of an outcome estimated to be caused by a specified exposure compared with a defined counterfactual. It cannot be obtained by dividing a global death figure among producers.
Persistence
How long a substance or its effects remain in the environment. Persistence alone does not establish the extent of an exposure or resulting injury.
Toxicity
The potential of a substance to cause adverse biological effects under particular conditions. Greenhouse-gas tonnage is not a toxicity ranking.
TERMS / EVIDENCE

Evidence, causation & due process

The source of a claim, the scientific inference and the legal decision are separate stages.

Primary source
An original dataset, official record, permit, judgment, research paper or observation that can be inspected directly for the stated claim.
Provenance
Information showing where a record came from, who published it, which date and version were used, and what transformations occurred.
Uncertainty
Limits in measurement, coverage or modeling that affect how much confidence a conclusion warrants. Unknown is not the same as a measured zero.
Confounder
A factor associated with both an exposure and an outcome that can distort an apparent causal relationship.
Counterfactual
A defined comparison describing what would have happened in the absence of the exposure or action being studied, used for causal attribution.
Legal entity
An organization recognized under an applicable legal system. A brand or corporate group name does not automatically identify the operator responsible for a specific site.
Effective-date ownership
Who owned or operated an entity or facility at a relevant date. Corporate ownership can change over time and must be documented before attribution.
Evidence gate
A documented stop or review requirement before escalating from a source observation to a stronger exposure, health or legal assertion.
Settlement
An agreement that resolves a dispute. Unless its terms or judicial record say otherwise, it should not be described as an admission of wrongdoing.
Legal liability
Responsibility under applicable law, requiring the relevant legal elements and decision-making process. An emissions quantity alone does not establish liability.
Remedy
Relief that may be available following an established claim, such as compensation, restoration or an injunction, depending on law and facts.
