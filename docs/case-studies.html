DOCUMENTED ENVIRONMENTAL MATTERS / 001—004

Evidence changes everything.

Four contrasting cases show how emissions become environmental pathways, health or ecological injury, remedies and contested legal records. They are not company Poison Index scores or a claim that every settlement constitutes an admission of wrongdoing.

01 — Chisso / Minamata disease

JAPAN · METHYLMERCURY · WATER → FOOD WEB → HUMANS
1956 discovery · 1968 official causation announcement · 1973 court compensation finding

Japan's environment ministry describes industrial methylmercury byproducts discharged in chemical-plant effluent, accumulating in seafood eaten by residents, with neurological disease as a documented endpoint. Its 1968 government assessment identified the Chisso Minamata plant as one source and a separate Showa Denko plant for the Niigata outbreak. The ministry's chronology reports a 1973 court decision recognizing Chisso's responsibility for compensation for damages. This is an example of a documented source-to-pathway-to-health-outcome case, not evidence of present-day discharges.

Research lesson: emissions data alone would have missed dietary transfer, latency, species-level concentration, clinical diagnosis, time-specific legal duties and the need to distinguish two separate sources.

02 — BP / Deepwater Horizon

UNITED STATES · OIL / MARINE ECOLOGY · WATER → ECOSYSTEMS / LIVELIHOODS
2010 event · ~134 million gallons released · 2016 $20.8B civil settlement

US Department of the Interior records describe the April 2010 Macondo well blowout, 11 worker fatalities and a release totaling approximately 134 million gallons over 87 days. A court approved a $20.8 billion settlement in April 2016 that addressed civil penalties and natural-resource damages. This was a specified oil-spill event with identifiable location, regulatory standards, injured natural resources and a structured restoration program.

Research lesson: quantify a specific event, map marine habitats and multiple injury endpoints, separate civil penalties from ecosystem restoration and do not count all settlement dollars as a direct measurement of biological harm.

03 — Vale / Brumadinho dam rupture

BRAZIL · MINE TAILINGS · LAND / WATER / HUMANS / COMMUNITIES
2019 rupture · 272 reported deaths · R$37.69B 2021 judicial reparations agreement

The Minas Gerais state government's official reparations portal reports that Vale's Córrego do Feijão mine dam failures on January 25, 2019 caused 272 deaths, with social, environmental and economic impacts in the Paraopeba River basin and beyond. Vale, state authorities, prosecutors and public defenders signed a judicial reparation agreement on February 4, 2021, valued at approximately R$37.69 billion. The government distinguishes collective reparations from potential individual civil or criminal proceedings.

Research lesson: separate instantaneous loss of life from sediment transport, habitat and river-basin effects, remediation milestones, social damages, legally distinct proceedings and the time horizon of continuing recovery.

04 — 3M / Public-water-supplier PFAS settlement

UNITED STATES · PFAS · DRINKING WATER → TREATMENT INFRASTRUCTURE
2024 final court approval · $10.5B–$12.5B nominal total payments

3M's September 2026 SEC-filed quarterly report says the US public-water-supplier settlement received court approval in March 2024 and became effective in May 2024. It describes total payments of $10.5–$12.5 billion. Importantly, the company states the settlement does not admit liability or wrongdoing. This matter concerns eligible public water suppliers and released claims; it is not a court determination that 3M caused a particular individual's disease.

Research lesson: distinguish settlement scope from scientific causality; water-supply remediation and monitoring from individual clinical injury; nominal scheduled payments from discounted accounting expense; and what a company acknowledges from what remains disputed.

05 — What POISONERS extracts from each case.

AxisEssential evidence
HistoricalEvent chronology, release years, ownership at the time, litigation timeline and later remediation.
AnnualYear-specific measured activity, concentrations, monitoring sites, injuries and remedial work.
FutureConditional residual-risk and restoration scenarios; no assumption of future violations.
ReceptorsAir, water, soil, plants, animals, humans — only the applicable and supportable pathways.
HazardsChemical identity/speciation, particulate, biological and physical hazards distinguished from climate gases.
LegalClaims filed, legal standard, identified parties, judgments, settlements, denials and remaining disputes.

These case studies illustrate different evidence structures. They are neither a curated list of the largest global harms nor a basis for extrapolating a numerical company-wide Poison Index without further primary data.

Publication rule: a legal settlement is not, by itself, proof of causation, an admission of liability or a quantification of injury. A final court decision, a reported corporate position, a regulator's conclusion and a scientific model require distinct labels.

i

Read the boundary, not just the number. The public company observations are production-linked 2024 climate data; the country atlas uses 2025 territorial estimates. Neither measures proven health injury, toxic-release impact or legal fault.
